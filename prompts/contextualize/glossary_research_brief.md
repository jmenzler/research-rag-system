# Synthesized research brief — cross-domain glossary

These briefs are generated by NotebookLM Deep Research from ~30-60 web
sources. Topic varies per brief (often quant finance, but also AI/ML, RAG,
crypto, social, infra). Domain-specific acronyms in finance-heavy briefs
are covered by `glossary_trading.md`; the items below are the cross-cutting
research / methodology vocabulary that recurs across briefs regardless of
topic.

## Research-methodology vocabulary
- RAG = Retrieval-Augmented Generation
- LLM = Large Language Model
- SOTA = state-of-the-art
- ablation = controlled removal of a component to measure its contribution
- benchmark = standardized eval; **dataset** = the corpus used to train/test
- baseline = the comparison point; **headline result** = top-line claim
- replication / reproducibility = independent re-run of a study
- meta-analysis = aggregation across multiple studies
- systematic review = structured survey of the literature
- preprint = un-peer-reviewed draft (arXiv, SSRN, bioRxiv)
- working paper = NBER/SSRN-style draft, not yet a journal article

## Brief-structure terms
- "Background" / "Context" — opening section, framing the question
- "Current state" / "State of X" — survey of what's already established
- "Key findings" / "Headline results" — synthesized claims
- "Open questions" / "Gaps" — what remains unresolved
- "Methodology" — how the cited studies establish their claims
- "Limitations" — caveats on the synthesized conclusions

## Citation shorthand
- "Smith et al. (2024)" — author-year, the canonical citation form briefs use
- [1], [2] — numeric citations the brief inlines, mapping to the source list
- "studies show" / "research suggests" — hedged synthesis claims (NOT
  primary results — flag these as synthesized when contextualizing)

For finance-specific terminology in briefs about market microstructure,
A-S, GLFT, VPIN, etc., defer to `glossary_trading.md` when those terms
actually appear in the chunk.
